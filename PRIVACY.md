# Privacy policy

_Calm Contraction Timer — last updated 21 November 2026_

Calm Contraction Timer ("the app") is an open-source Android app for timing contractions.
This policy explains what data the app handles. The short version: **your contraction data
stays on your phone.**

## Contraction data

- Start and end times, intensity and notes you enter are stored only in a database on your
  phone.
- The app has no server and no account. The developers never receive your data.
- Android backup and device-to-device transfer are switched off for this data.
- The data leaves your phone only when **you** export a CSV file or PDF report and share
  it through the Android share menu. The exported file is kept in the app's private cache
  only until your next export, **Delete all contractions**, or the next app start.
- The app contains no analytics, advertising or tracking code and never sends contraction
  data over the network.
- Uninstalling the app, or using **Settings → Delete all contractions**, removes it from your
  phone.

## Settings

Preferences such as theme, pattern setting and the phone numbers you save are stored on your
phone only.

## Purchases

The optional one-time Pro unlock is sold through Google Play. Google Play processes the
payment; the app only learns whether this Google account owns Pro, and remembers that on your
phone. The app never sees your payment details, and no contraction data is involved. Payments
are covered by Google Play's own terms and [privacy policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy).

## Notifications

During a session the app shows a notification with a timer and Start/Stop buttons. It is
created on your phone and is not sent through any server.

## Children

The app is intended for adults and is not directed at children.

## Changes

If a future version adds features that handle data differently (for example optional
advertising or a cloud backup you start yourself), this policy will be updated
before that version is released. Changes to this policy are published in this file in the
app's public repository, with the date at the top.
